Use this checklist at the end of each month, or after each payout if you process many orders. It is written for store owners and for the bookkeepers who close their books. The core idea is simple: reconcile payout by payout, not month by month, because a payout is the unit your bank actually sees.

## Before you start

- Pick the period and note the time zone your store reports in. Shopify's cut-off for a day may not match your accounting month end.
- Gather the Shopify orders, the payout details and the bank statement for the same period.
- List every payment gateway the store uses. Orders paid with Stripe, PayPal or a buy-now-pay-later provider settle through their own payouts, not through Shopify Payments.

## Checks for each payout

- The payout total equals the deposit on your bank statement.
- The payout breaks down into charges, fees, refunds, adjustments and chargebacks, and those lines add up to the total.
- Every charge in the payout maps to a Shopify order.
- Refunds in the payout match refunds recorded in Shopify.
- Any difference has a reason you can write down: timing, a fee, a refund, a dispute or an adjustment.

## Checks across the whole period

- Every order marked Paid has a matching payment in some gateway. Orders marked paid with no payment are the ones worth chasing first.
- No order has been charged twice.
- Refunds issued in Shopify show up in the gateway, and the reverse.
- Chargebacks and disputes are recorded once, with the fee.
- Orders near the period boundary are in the right month.

## If you use accounting software

- Record each payout as one entry that matches the bank deposit. Many bookkeepers use a clearing account for this and check that it returns to zero after reconciliation.
- Confirm fees and refunds are recorded separately from sales.
- Keep the payout report with the entry as support.

## Where Fincile fits

Fincile checks the payments behind your Shopify orders. It compares orders marked paid against Stripe, PayPal or a payout CSV and lists missing payments, duplicate charges, chargebacks and failed refunds. It does not post entries to QuickBooks or Xero, and it does not change your books. Use it before you post, so the entries you record are based on payments that exist.
